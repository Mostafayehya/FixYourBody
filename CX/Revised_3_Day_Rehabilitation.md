# Revised 3-Day/Week Knee Rehabilitation Program

**Status:** Recommended discussion draft based on the previous review; validate exercise selection, intensity, and range with the treating sports physiotherapist.

**Focus:** Progressive quadriceps strength; hamstring and gluteal strength; knee control with reported hypermobility; calf/ankle stability; functional standing, walking and step-down tolerance.

> **Key context:** Previously reported knee hypermobility and left posterior-medial discomfort do not necessarily arise from chondromalacia alone. A clinician should check whether the working diagnosis explains the pain pattern.
>
> **Safety:** Do not force knee hyperextension. A conservative initial symptom rule is ≤3/10 knee pain during exercise and no increase in symptoms the following morning. Pain rules are guides, not diagnostic tests. Stop for sharp pain, new swelling, true locking, or significant giving way; seek professional review. Do not use generated illustrations as a substitute for technique coaching.

**How to use the schedule:** Treat the listed sets as starting targets, **not mandatory minimums**. Keep 1–3 repetitions in reserve on familiar machine exercises when well tolerated; allow more margin on unfamiliar exercises. Rest about 1–2 minutes between moderate sets, longer when needed to preserve quality. Warm up and use comfortable, controlled joint ranges.

## Progression rules

1. **Quality first:** Control each repetition without pain escalation, instability, or forced hyperextension.
2. **Assess the 24-hour response:** If pain, swelling or next-morning symptoms exceed baseline, reduce load, range, sets, or exercise difficulty.
3. **Progress one factor at a time:** When the full prescribed rep range feels controlled over consecutive sessions, consider a small load increase (e.g. 2.5–5% when appropriate) or a modest repetition increase.
4. **RPE by exercise:** Established leg press/leg extension often RPE 6–8; hip thrust and RDL RPE 6–7; novel balance/step movements prioritize technical control, roughly RPE 5–7. Assisted Nordics should not be performed to failure.
5. **Maintain movement options:** Full comfortable knee extension is not inherently forbidden, but avoid locking backward into uncontrolled hyperextension.

## What to review with the physiotherapist

- Whether chondromalacia explains the **posterior-medial** pain, or whether additional assessment is indicated.
- Whether the leg extension / leg press training volume should match or gradually approach your previously tolerated sessions.
- How to dose Nordic curls in light of your positive symptom response and prior exercise history.
- Which knee extension and flexion ranges are comfortable **without uncontrolled hyperextension**.
